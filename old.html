PortionVision

"An intelligent nutrition system for hostel mess environments that combines personalized portion recommendations with image-based portion estimation for accurate comparison."

Problem Statement

In hostel mess environments, food is self-served without structured guidance on portion sizes. Students differ in age, height, weight, metabolism, activity level, and health goals, leading to guesswork and poor nutritional tracking. Furthermore, estimating food portion weights from a single RGB image is inherently challenging due to the lack of depth information, especially for Indian dishes lacking standardized sizes.

How We Solve It

PortionVision digitizes the daily mess menu, computes personalized nutritional requirements based on user biometric profiles, and recommends precise portions. It leverages a Computer Vision pipeline to analyze a photo of the user's plate, estimating real-world mass through depth-based volume integration and density mapping (currently under calibration) to provide an "Ideal vs. Actual" consumption feedback loop.

System Architecture

Backend Systems

Working
  • Tesseract.js OCR dynamically maps unstructured menu text to the JSON food database.
  • Recommendation engine filters dishes by diet to compute ideal portions.

CV Pipeline

Validating
  • Depth Anything estimates relative depth maps while SAM generates segmentation masks used for downstream volumetric computation.
  • Region-based classification using MobileNetV3 produces food label predictions with confidence scores.
  • A Hungarian optimal assignment algorithm maps segmented regions to expected food items for accurate matching.
  • Pipeline is fully integrated; physical mass estimation is undergoing final calibration.

CV Pipeline Deep Dive

1. Depth & Segmentation

Input images pass a quality gate (blur and tilt detection) before entering the pipeline. Depth Anything estimates relative depth maps while SAM generates binary masks used for later volumetric computation.

2. Calibration & Classification

Plate scale calibration converts pixels to cm. Food regions are classified using MobileNetV3, with OCR-extracted menu items boosting prediction confidence.

3. Volumetric Mass

Voxel-wise integration computes volume using depth maps, fused with food-specific height priors. Dynamic density mapping converts the volume to estimated mass (grams).

A composite confidence score combines segmentation, classification, depth stability, and calibration reliability.

Current Status

Prototype Stage: The system is currently at a prototype stage, with CV-based estimation requiring further calibration for reliable deployment.

Model Limitations

While the CV pipeline handles structured plates well, there are inherent challenges:

  • Monocular Ambiguity: Single-view depth estimation relies on visual priors; without multi-view geometry, a flat roti can exhibit depth signals mathematically similar to a shallow dal.
  • Segmentation Overlap: Deep learning models like SAM struggle with semantically blended edges, such as dal poured directly over rice, causing volume leakage between regions.
  • Scale Dependency: Accurate mass estimation heavily relies on standard plate geometries. Uncalibrated frames heavily skew volume results.

Future Work

Custom Weights

Fine-tuning models explicitly on an Indian Mess Meals dataset to boost accuracy for closely related foods like distinct curries.

Cutlery Filtering

Adding a structural detection layer to identify and ignore forks, spoons, and non-food items to prevent volume estimation inflation.

Real-Time AR

Migrating to cloud GPU edge endpoints to project recommended portion overlay targets directly via mobile WebGL in augmented reality.
