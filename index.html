PortionVision Indian Food Identification for Automated Portion Estimation

Problem Statement

In hostel mess environments, food is self-served without structured guidance on portion sizes. Students differ in age, height, weight, metabolism, activity level, and health goals, leading to guesswork and poor nutritional tracking. Furthermore, estimating food portion weights from a single RGB image is inherently challenging due to the lack of depth information, especially for Indian dishes lacking standardized sizes.

The earlier PBL project established the broader pipeline — including OCR for menu and database, personalized recommendations, segmentation and depth-based mass estimation — but a major bottleneck remained reliable food identification, particularly for Indian dishes.Visually similar dishes and the eventual need for multi-food recognition make this a key challenge.

Objectives

  • Build a reliable Indian food classifier for mess and hostel images.
  • Improve the food-identification stage of the existing PortionVision pipeline.
  • Establish a foundation for future multi-food portion and nutrition estimation.

Literature / Existing Work

Previous PBL work

  • Built the overall PortionVision pipeline with OCR, personalized nutrition logic, and plate analysis components.
  • Identified the key limitation: the classifier was not sufficiently accurate for real Indian food categories.
  • Recognized that a robust food recognition module is essential before mass estimation can be trusted.

Relevant computer vision trends

  • Transfer learning with ImageNet-pretrained CNNs is standard for food recognition tasks.
  • EfficientNet provides a strong balance between accuracy and model size.
  • Food datasets are often imbalanced, and visually similar dishes remain a known challenge in classification.
  • Real-world food recognition is harder than benchmark datasets because of angle, lighting, clutter, and mixed plates.

Methodology

1. Dataset

Khana Dataset: approximately 82,000 images across 80 Indian food categories with uneven class distribution.

2. Split

70/15/15 train-validation-test split using a fixed random seed for reproducible evaluation.

3. Preprocessing

224×224 input with ImageNet normalization; random crop, flip, colour jitter, and rotation during training.

4. Model

EfficientNet-B0 pretrained on ImageNet with the classifier head replaced for 80 classes.

5. Training

AdamW, differential learning rates (1e-5 backbone / 1e-4 head), label smoothing (0.1), and cosine learning-rate scheduling.

6. Evaluation

Top-1/Top-5 accuracy, precision, recall, macro F1, confusion matrix, Grad-CAM, and real-image prediction.

Problems & Limitations

Classifier Problems

  • Class imbalance affects low-support categories.
  • Visually similar foods cause confusion.
  • Lighting, angle, and presentation affect predictions.
  • The current model is single-label and has no explicit unknown class.

Overall PortionVision Problems

  • Monocular depth introduces volume-estimation uncertainty.
  • Plate geometry and calibration affect mass estimation.

Plan for Remaining Work

  • Collect more labelled data for Indian food classification to improve recall, class coverage, and robustness.
  • Reduce confusion among visually similar dishes and improve real-image prediction confidence.
  • Expand the classifier toward multi-food thali recognition instead of single-label prediction.
  • Improve calibration and depth estimation for more reliable volume and mass measurement.
  • Integrate food recognition with segmentation, depth, and mass estimation for final deployment in hostel and mess environments.

Conclusion

The earlier PortionVision project established the broader system architecture, but this semester focused on a major missing component: reliable Indian food classification. By building a transfer-learning-based classifier with strong evaluation metrics and real-image prediction capability, we have created a solid foundation for the final portion estimation system. The next phase is to expand beyond the current class scope, improve weak classes, and integrate the recognition stage into full multi-food meal analysis.
